1. Pinhole + Radial-Tangential (Brown-Conrady)

Paper: D.C. Brown, "Decentering Distortion of Lenses," Photogrammetric Engineering, 1966.
Used by: OpenCV (default), COLMAP, ORB-SLAM2/3, Kalibr, MVIS
FOV: Up to ~140°

Forward Projection (3D → 2D)

Step 1 — Perspective division:

$$x = \frac{X}{Z}, \quad y = \frac{Y}{Z}, \quad r^2 = x^2 + y^2$$

Step 2 — Radial distortion:

$$d_r = 1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4$$

Step 3 — Tangential distortion:

$$\Delta x = 2 p_1 x y + p_2 (r^2 + 2x^2)$$ $$\Delta y = p_1 (r^2 + 2y^2) + 2 p_2 x y$$

Step 4 — Distorted coordinates + pixel mapping:

$$x_d = x \cdot d_r + \Delta x, \quad y_d = y \cdot d_r + \Delta y$$ $$u = f_x \cdot x_d + c_x, \quad v = f_y \cdot y_d + c_y$$

Parameters (8)

ParameterMeaning
$f_x, f_y$Focal length (pixels)
$c_x, c_y$Principal point (pixels)
$k_1, k_2$Radial distortion coefficients
$p_1, p_2$Tangential (decentering) distortion

Unprojection (Iterative)

Initialize: $x_0 = \frac{u - c_x}{f_x}, \; y_0 = \frac{v - c_y}{f_y}$

Iterate until convergence:

$$x_{i+1} = \frac{x_0 - \Delta x(x_i, y_i)}{d_r(x_i, y_i)}, \quad y_{i+1} = \frac{y_0 - \Delta y(x_i, y_i)}{d_r(x_i, y_i)}$$

Typically converges in 5–20 iterations.

Jacobian

Let $D = \frac{\partial d_r}{\partial r^2} = k_1 + 2k_2 r^2$. Then:

$$\frac{\partial x_d}{\partial x} = d_r + 2x^2 D + 2p_1 y + 6p_2 x$$ $$\frac{\partial x_d}{\partial y} = 2xy D + 2p_1 x + 2p_2 y$$ $$\frac{\partial y_d}{\partial x} = 2xy D + 2p_1 x + 2p_2 y$$ $$\frac{\partial y_d}{\partial y} = d_r + 2y^2 D + 6p_1 y + 2p_2 x$$

Full Jacobian: $\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(X,Y,Z)} = \text{diag}(f_x, f_y) \cdot \mathbf{J}_\text{dist} \cdot \mathbf{J}_\text{persp}$

where $\mathbf{J}_\text{persp} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/Z & 0 & -X/Z^2 \\ 0 & 1/Z & -Y/Z^2 \end{bmatrix}$

2. Equidistant (Kannala-Brandt polynomial on x/z)

Paper: J. Kannala & S. Brandt, "A Generic Camera Model and Calibration Method for Conventional, Wide-Angle, and Fish-Eye Lenses," IEEE TPAMI, 2006.
Used by: OpenCV fisheye, Basalt, ORB-SLAM3, Kalibr, MVIS
FOV: Up to ~195°

Forward Projection

Step 1 — Incidence angle:

$$\theta = \arctan\left(\frac{\sqrt{X^2 + Y^2}}{Z}\right), \quad \phi = \arctan2(Y, X)$$

Step 2 — Distorted angle (odd-power polynomial):

$$\theta_d = \theta + k_1 \theta^3 + k_2 \theta^5 + k_3 \theta^7 + k_4 \theta^9$$

Step 3 — Project:

$$x_d = \theta_d \cos\phi, \quad y_d = \theta_d \sin\phi$$

(equivalently, $x_d = \frac{\theta_d}{r} x$, $y_d = \frac{\theta_d}{r} y$ where $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$)

Step 4 — Pixel coordinates:

$$u = f_x \cdot x_d + c_x, \quad v = f_y \cdot y_d + c_y$$

Parameters (8)

ParameterMeaning
$f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$Intrinsics
$k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4$Angular distortion coefficients

Unprojection (Newton's method)

Given pixel $(u,v)$, compute $\theta_d = \sqrt{x_d^2 + y_d^2}$, $\phi = \arctan2(y_d, x_d)$.

Solve for $\theta$ via Newton iteration:

$$f(\theta) = \theta + k_1\theta^3 + k_2\theta^5 + k_3\theta^7 + k_4\theta^9 - \theta_d$$ $$f'(\theta) = 1 + 3k_1\theta^2 + 5k_2\theta^4 + 7k_3\theta^6 + 9k_4\theta^8$$ $$\theta_{i+1} = \theta_i - \frac{f(\theta_i)}{f'(\theta_i)}, \quad \theta_0 = \theta_d$$

Then: $\mathbf{r} = [\sin\theta\cos\phi, \; \sin\theta\sin\phi, \; \cos\theta]^\top$

Jacobian

Let $s = \theta_d / r$ (scaling), $D' = \frac{\partial\theta_d}{\partial\theta} = 1 + 3k_1\theta^2 + 5k_2\theta^4 + 7k_3\theta^6 + 9k_4\theta^8$:

$$\frac{\partial x_d}{\partial x} = s + \frac{x^2}{r^2}\left(\frac{D'}{1+r^2} - s\right)$$ $$\frac{\partial x_d}{\partial y} = \frac{xy}{r^2}\left(\frac{D'}{1+r^2} - s\right)$$

KB4 — four radial terms, θ from atan2 (distortion_model: kb4)

cal_vec [fx, fy, cx, cy, k1, k2, k3, k4] (8 parameters), the same as Basalt's kb4:

$$\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(\sqrt{X^2+Y^2},\, Z), \qquad \theta_d = \theta(1 + k_1\theta^2 + k_2\theta^4 + k_3\theta^6 + k_4\theta^8)$$ $$u = f_x \theta_d \frac{X}{\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}} + c_x, \qquad v = f_y \theta_d \frac{Y}{\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}} + c_y$$

Against equidistant. The same polynomial, a different domain. equidistant (gtsam Cal3Fisheye, Kalibr pinhole-equi) divides by $Z$ first, so it stops at 90°; kb4 does not. Below 90° the two kernels agree to 1e-9 px. That is also why a kb4 camera exports to Kalibr as pinhole-equi with the same coefficients: exact in front of the camera, with no pixel for a ray at or past 90°, and the export warns when the fit used such corners or the fold lies inside the image. The generic names fisheye and kannala_brandt mean kb4; only equidistant itself selects equidistant. Solves are not bit-identical, because the camera-only pose Jacobian and the initial inverse differ.

Valid domain. $0 \le \theta < \min(\pi, \theta_\text{fold})$, where $\theta_\text{fold}$ is the first positive root of $1 + 3k_1x + 5k_2x^2 + 7k_3x^3 + 9k_4x^4$ with $x = \theta^2$. Past it, projection fails and pixels unproject to invalid, never to a wrong ray. A fold does not stop a solve. corner_accounting in the result counts the corners past it, and the fold level is none (fold absent or outside the image), warn (inside the image, past the data, or $\theta$ reaches $\pi$ inside the image) or alarm (observed corners past the fold). In cam-IMU such a corner has a zero residual, so it lowers the cost rather than raising it.

Reported domain. Pixels farther than $R_{fold} = \min(f_x, f_y)\,\theta_d(\theta_{fold})$ from $(c_x, c_y)$ have no ray. After every kb4 solve (camera-only and cam-IMU) MVIS computes $\theta_{fold}$ and $R_{fold}$, logs them, adds them to the text / HTML reports and to the output yaml as camera metadata valid_domain (fold_theta_rad, max_theta_rad, max_radius_px, corner_radius_px, covers_image; readers ignore it, it is not a required key), and logs a WARNING — not a guardrail failure — when $R_{fold}$ is below the image-corner radius (the farthest image corner from $(c_x, c_y)$). Reports tag that case FOLDS INSIDE IMAGE when a real fold ends the domain, and VALID DOMAIN ENDS INSIDE IMAGE when $\theta_d$ is monotonic and only $R(\pi)$ falls short of the corner (a short focal, no fold). Kernels: custom_projections.project_kb / unproject_kb.

Pass-2 revert. When the pass-2 guardrail fires, the revert is rig-wide for every model: pass 2 is one joint solve, so one camera's pass-1 intrinsics cannot be spliced into the pass-2 extrinsics.

Limits. Single solve from the equidistant init. PnP keeps its 80° ray limit, and feature mode stays front-hemisphere. The Kalibr export is pinhole-equi (above), so rays at or past 90° have no pixel there. --undistort refuses it. Basalt has no fold check, so parity with Basalt holds on in-domain rays only.

Jacobian sketch (analytic; finite-difference checked for point and intrinsics, near-axis and $\theta > \pi/2$ included). On the optical axis ($\rho < 10^{-9}\|\mathbf P\|$) $s = \theta_d/\rho \to 1/Z$ (series limit).

With $D' = d\theta_d/d\theta = 1 + \sum_i (2i+1) k_i \theta^{2i}$, $d\theta/d\rho = Z/\|\mathbf P\|^2$ and $d\theta/dZ = -\rho/\|\mathbf P\|^2$:

$$\frac{\partial s}{\partial \rho} = \frac{D'\, Z/\|\mathbf P\|^2 - s}{\rho}, \qquad \frac{\partial s}{\partial Z} = -\frac{D'}{\|\mathbf P\|^2}$$ $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial X} = f_x\left(s + \frac{X^2}{\rho}\frac{\partial s}{\partial\rho}\right), \quad \frac{\partial u}{\partial Y} = f_x \frac{XY}{\rho}\frac{\partial s}{\partial\rho}, \quad \frac{\partial u}{\partial Z} = f_x X \frac{\partial s}{\partial Z}$$ $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial k_i} = f_x \frac{X}{\rho}\, \theta^{2i+1}, \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial f_x} = s X, \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial c_x} = 1 \quad (\text{same for } v)$$

Unprojection with the fold bracket.

$r_d = \sqrt{m_x^2 + m_y^2}$ with $m = ((u-c_x)/f_x,\ (v-c_y)/f_y)$. Solve $\theta_d(\theta) = r_d$ by Newton from $\theta_0 = \min(r_d, \theta_{fold}/2)$, safeguarded by bisection on $[0, \theta_{fold})$, so the root never lands on a folded (aliasing) branch; no convergence inside the bracket (e.g. $r_d > \theta_d(\theta_{fold})$) means no ray. Then $\mathbf r = [\sin\theta\, m_x/r_d,\; \sin\theta\, m_y/r_d,\; \cos\theta]^\top$.

3. FOV Model (Devernay & Faugeras)

Paper: F. Devernay & O. Faugeras, "Straight lines have to be straight," Machine Vision and Applications, 2001.
Used by: LSD-SLAM, DSO, Kalibr
FOV: Up to ~180°

Forward Projection

$$r_u = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$$ $$r_d = \frac{1}{\omega} \arctan\!\left(2\, r_u \tan\frac{\omega}{2}\right)$$ $$x_d = \frac{r_d}{r_u}\, x, \quad y_d = \frac{r_d}{r_u}\, y$$ $$u = f_x\, x_d + c_x, \quad v = f_y\, y_d + c_y$$

When $\omega \to 0$, $r_d / r_u \to 1$ (reduces to pinhole).

Parameters (5)

ParameterMeaning
$f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$Intrinsics
$\omega$FOV parameter (radians). Single distortion parameter.

Unprojection (Closed-form)

$$r_d = \sqrt{x_d^2 + y_d^2}$$ $$r_u = \frac{\tan(r_d \cdot \omega)}{2\,\tan(\omega/2)}$$ $$x = \frac{r_u}{r_d}\, x_d, \quad y = \frac{r_u}{r_d}\, y_d$$
Advantage: Closed-form unprojection — no iteration needed. Simplest model with only 1 distortion parameter.

4. Double Sphere (DS)

Paper: V. Usenko, N. Demmel, D. Cremers, "The Double Sphere Camera Model," 3DV, 2018.
Used by: Basalt (default), Granite
FOV: Up to ~195°

Forward Projection

$$d_1 = \sqrt{X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2}$$ $$d_2 = \sqrt{X^2 + Y^2 + (\xi\, d_1 + Z)^2}$$ $$w = \alpha\, d_2 + (1 - \alpha)(\xi\, d_1 + Z)$$ $$u = f_x \frac{X}{w} + c_x, \quad v = f_y \frac{Y}{w} + c_y$$

Parameters (6)

ParameterMeaningRange
$f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$Intrinsics—
$\xi$First sphere parameter$[-1, 1]$
$\alpha$Second sphere parameter$[0, 1]$

Unprojection (Closed-form)

$$m_x = \frac{u - c_x}{f_x}, \quad m_y = \frac{v - c_y}{f_y}, \quad r^2 = m_x^2 + m_y^2$$ $$m_z = \frac{1 - \alpha^2 r^2}{\alpha\sqrt{1 - (2\alpha - 1)r^2} + 1 - \alpha}$$

Valid when $1 - (2\alpha - 1)r^2 > 0$.

$$\lambda = \frac{m_z\,\xi + \sqrt{m_z^2 + (1-\xi^2)r^2}}{m_z^2 + r^2}$$ $$\mathbf{r} = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda\, m_x \\ \lambda\, m_y \\ \lambda\, m_z - \xi \end{bmatrix}$$
Key advantage: Both projection AND unprojection are closed-form. No iteration anywhere. Fastest Jacobians. Recommended for real-time SLAM by Basalt.

Jacobian $\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(X,Y,Z)}$

Let $w = \alpha d_2 + (1-\alpha)(\xi d_1 + Z)$:

$$\frac{\partial d_1}{\partial X} = \frac{X}{d_1}, \quad \frac{\partial d_2}{\partial X} = \frac{X(1 + \xi(\xi d_1 + Z)/d_1)}{d_2}$$ $$\frac{\partial w}{\partial X} = \alpha\frac{\partial d_2}{\partial X} + (1-\alpha)\frac{\xi X}{d_1}$$ $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial X} = f_x \frac{w - X\frac{\partial w}{\partial X}}{w^2}$$

(analogous for $\partial u/\partial Y$, $\partial u/\partial Z$, and $v$ derivatives)

5. Extended Unified Camera Model (EUCM)

Paper: B. Khomutenko, G. Garcia, P. Music, "An Enhanced Unified Camera Model," IEEE RA-L, 2016.
Used by: Basalt, Kalibr (via extensions)
FOV: Up to ~195°

Forward Projection

$$d = \sqrt{\beta(X^2 + Y^2) + Z^2}$$ $$w = \alpha\, d + (1 - \alpha) Z$$ $$u = f_x \frac{X}{w} + c_x, \quad v = f_y \frac{Y}{w} + c_y$$

Parameters (6)

ParameterMeaning
$f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$Intrinsics
$\alpha$Blend parameter ($0$=pinhole, $1$=omnidirectional)
$\beta$Sphere shape ($1$=sphere, $\neq 1$=ellipsoid)

Unprojection (Closed-form)

$$m_x = \frac{u - c_x}{f_x}, \quad m_y = \frac{v - c_y}{f_y}, \quad r^2 = m_x^2 + m_y^2, \quad \rho^2 = \beta\, r^2$$ $$m_z = \frac{1 - \alpha^2 \rho^2}{\alpha\sqrt{1 - (2\alpha-1)\rho^2} + 1 - \alpha}$$ $$\lambda = \frac{m_z\,\alpha + \sqrt{m_z^2 + (1-\alpha^2)\rho^2}}{m_z^2 + \rho^2}$$ $$\mathbf{r} = \begin{bmatrix}\lambda\, m_x \\ \lambda\, m_y \\ \lambda\, m_z - \alpha\end{bmatrix}$$
When $\beta=1$, EUCM reduces to the Unified Camera Model (UCM/Mei) with $\xi = \alpha/(1-\alpha)$.

UCM in MVIS (distortion_model: ucm)

MVIS stores the UCM in the $\alpha$ form, as Basalt ucm does: cal_vec [fx, fy, cx, cy, alpha], projection as above with $\beta = 1$ (so ucm $\equiv$ eucm($\beta=1$) $\equiv$ double_sphere($\xi=0$)). It is a separate model with 5 parameters, not EUCM with $\beta$ pinned.

$$\xi = \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}, \qquad \gamma_{u,v} = \frac{f_{x,y}}{1-\alpha} \qquad (\text{Kalibr } \texttt{omni-none}: [\xi, \gamma_u, \gamma_v, c_x, c_y])$$
  • Validity (Basalt): a point projects iff $Z > -w\,d$ with $w = (1-\alpha)/\alpha$ for $\alpha > 0.5$, else $\alpha/(1-\alpha)$; at $\alpha = 0.64$ the fold is at ~124° incidence. A pixel unprojects iff $\alpha \le 0.5$ or $r^2 < 1/(2\alpha-1)$.
  • Range: $\alpha \in [0, 1)$ ($\alpha = 1$ is $\xi = \infty$). A result outside it is flagged, and no Kalibr camchain is written for it (the MVIS yaml still is).
  • Kalibr: an omni + none camchain imports as ucm; ucm exports as omni + none. The Kalibr RS tool has no omni-none, so ucm is global-shutter only there.
  • Aliases: unified, omni_none, omni-none. Bare omni / mei stay omni_radtan.

6. Unified Camera Model / Omni (Mei)

Paper: C. Mei & P. Rives, "Single View Point Omnidirectional Camera Calibration from Planar Grids," ICRA, 2007.
Foundation: C. Geyer & K. Daniilidis, "A Unifying Theory for Central Catadioptric Systems," ECCV, 2000.
Used by: Kalibr (omni), OKVIS, MVIS (distortion_model: omni_radtan, coeffs [xi, k1, k2, p1, p2]; legacy 4-coeff files load with $p_2=0$)
FOV: Up to ~360° (catadioptric)

p2 (Kalibr parity). MVIS estimates the full Kalibr omni-radtan set: cal_vec [fx, fy, cx, cy, xi, k1, k2, p1, p2] (9 parameters; $J_{cal}$ is $2\times 9$). $p_2$ is always free, with the existing camera-only schedule (phase 1: $\xi$ under a loose prior, $k_1, k_2, p_1, p_2$ pinned; phase 2: $\xi$ frozen, $k_1, k_2, p_1, p_2$ free). Kalibr camchains import and export the real $p_2$; legacy 4-coeff MVIS / Kalibr files and 8-long cal_vecs load with $p_2 = 0$.

Forward Projection

Step 1 — Sphere + shifted projection:

$$d = \|\mathbf{P}\| = \sqrt{X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2}$$ $$x_s = \frac{X}{Z + \xi\, d}, \quad y_s = \frac{Y}{Z + \xi\, d}$$

Step 2 — Optional radial-tangential distortion:

$$r^2 = x_s^2 + y_s^2$$ $$x_d = x_s(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4) + 2p_1 x_s y_s + p_2(r^2 + 2x_s^2)$$ $$y_d = y_s(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4) + p_1(r^2 + 2y_s^2) + 2p_2 x_s y_s$$

Step 3 — Pixel:

$$u = f_x\, x_d + c_x, \quad v = f_y\, y_d + c_y$$

Parameters (9)

ParameterMeaning
$f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$Intrinsics
$\xi$Mirror/unification param ($\xi=0$: pinhole, $\xi=1$: parabolic)
$k_1, k_2, p_1, p_2$Distortion (applied after sphere projection)

Unprojection (Closed-form for UCM part)

After removing distortion (iterative if $k_1,k_2,p_1,p_2$ present):

$$r^2 = x_s^2 + y_s^2$$ $$\lambda = \frac{\xi + \sqrt{1 + (1-\xi^2)r^2}}{1 + r^2}$$ $$\mathbf{r} = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda\, x_s \\ \lambda\, y_s \\ \lambda - \xi \end{bmatrix}$$

7. Special Sensor Types

Event Cameras (DVS/DAVIS)

Same projection models as frame cameras. Event cameras use identical silicon pixel arrays and optics. The difference is temporal (asynchronous per-pixel brightness change), not spatial. Calibration uses blinking patterns or coexisting grayscale frames (DAVIS).

Ref: Mueggler et al., "Continuous-Time Visual-Inertial Odometry for Event Cameras," IEEE T-RO, 2018.

Thermal / IR Cameras (FLIR, LWIR)

Same projection models. Germanium/chalcogenide lenses follow the same geometric optics. Special considerations: thermal drift of focal length, narcissus effect on principal point, and need for thermal-contrast calibration targets (heated checkerboard, metal plates with holes).

Ref: Vidas et al., "A Mask-Based Approach for Geometric Calibration of Thermal-Infrared Cameras," IEEE T-IM, 2012.

Underwater Cameras

Refractive model needed for flat ports. Snell’s law at water-glass-air interfaces creates a non-single-viewpoint camera. Dome ports centered on the entrance pupil preserve SVP.

Flat port refraction (Snell’s law):

$$n_\text{water}\sin\theta_\text{water} = n_\text{air}\sin\theta_\text{air}$$

where $n_\text{water} \approx 1.33$. For narrow FOV, absorbed into modified intrinsics.

Ref: Treibitz et al., "Flat Refractive Geometry," IEEE TPAMI, 2012.

Microscope Cameras (Telecentric)

Orthographic projection (no perspective foreshortening):

$$u = m_x X + c_x, \quad v = m_y Y + c_y$$

No division by $Z$ — objects at different depths have the same apparent size.

8. Model Selection Guide

Choose by the largest incidence angle θ of the board corners on the camera (off the optical axis), not by the lens's advertised field of view. After a solve, check corner_accounting and valid_domain in the result: they report the largest θ used and any corners past the model's domain.

Against EUCM the KB models differ on T265 by up to 1.7e-3 rad and 1.6 mm in cam-IMU rotation and lever arm; about half of that comes from board-shape estimation. DS has closed-form projection and unprojection, which some real-time systems (Basalt) use.
